**---How does the transition from Hadoop 1 to Hadoop 2 affect MapReduce architecture?***

The transition from Hadoop 1 to Hadoop 2 significantly transformed the MapReduce architecture by introducing a new layer called YARN (Yet Another Resource Negotiator). This shift changed the fundamental processing model and the daemons responsible for executing jobs.

***...Key Architectural Changes...***

• Introduction of YARN: In Hadoop 1, MapReduce was the primary processing framework situated directly on top of HDFS. In Hadoop 2, MapReduce was removed as the direct layer and replaced by YARN, which acts as a centralized cluster manager. This allows multiple frameworks—not just MapReduce, but also Spark, Storm, and HBase—to run on a common execution model.

• Evolution of Daemons: The daemons used in Hadoop 1, known as the JobTracker and TaskTracker, were replaced in Hadoop 2 by the ResourceManager and NodeManager. While JobTracker and TaskTracker were exclusive to MapReduce, the new ResourceManager and NodeManager serve any technology running on YARN.

• Separation of Responsibilities: In the traditional Hadoop 1 architecture, the JobTracker suffered from intense resource pressure because it was responsible for all aspects of a job, including cluster monitoring, resource allocation, scheduling, and execution. Hadoop 2 alleviated this pressure by splitting these tasks: the ResourceManager handles global resource allocation, while a specific ApplicationMaster is created for every individual job to manage its task scheduling and monitoring
